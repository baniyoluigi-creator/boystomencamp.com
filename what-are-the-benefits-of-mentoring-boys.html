A Question We Hear Often

What are the benefits of mentoring boys?

Mentoring works on a boy from several directions at once. Here is what it actually gives him that home alone often cannot.

Parents sometimes ask us this before they have even decided their son needs help. They just want to understand what mentoring actually does.

The truth is, mentoring works on a boy from several directions at once, not just one.

It gives him a male voice outside his own home, someone who can say the same things a parent says, but in a way he is more ready to hear.

“It is the mentor's steady presence, week after week, that slowly builds the boy up from several sides at once.”

It gives him real examples to copy, not just words about good character, but a grown man actually living it in front of him.

It gives him a safe place to be wrong, to try something, fail, and try again, without feeling like he has let his family down.

And it gives him language for his own feelings, because most boys were never taught how to name what is happening inside them.

We have watched quiet boys become boys who can stand and speak. We have watched angry boys become boys who can explain what hurt them instead of lashing out. No single lesson does that. It is the mentor's steady presence, week after week, session after session, that slowly builds the boy up from several sides at once.

If you have been wondering whether mentoring is worth it, this is usually what it looks like from the inside, small changes stacking on top of each other until one day you notice your son standing differently.

Boys to Men Mentorship Camp was built around exactly this kind of steady, layered mentoring.
