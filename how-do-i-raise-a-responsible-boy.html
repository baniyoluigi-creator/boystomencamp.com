A Question We Hear Often

How do I raise a responsible boy?

Responsibility cannot be lectured into a boy. It has to be practiced, and it usually fails for a few specific, repeatable reasons. Here they are.

Parents ask me this all the time. Usually it is right after they find out their son said he did his homework, but he did not. Or he left a job half done and called it finished.

Here is something I have learned after many years of working with boys. You cannot lecture a boy into being responsible. Responsibility is like a muscle. It only grows when it is used again and again, under real conditions, not under constant warnings.

Most boys who struggle with responsibility were never really allowed to fail and feel it. Somewhere along the way, an adult stepped in too early. They fixed the forgotten homework. They finished the chore he left undone. The boy learns, without anyone meaning to teach it, that someone will always catch what he drops.

“Responsibility is like a muscle. It only grows when it is used again and again, under real conditions, not under constant warnings.”

At camp, we do the opposite, on purpose. If a boy forgets his part in a task, the whole group feels it. If he does not prepare, he stands in front of others without being ready. We are not trying to embarrass any boy. But we let the natural result of his choice teach him, with a mentor close by afterward to help him understand what happened and why.

At home, the change can be small. Give your son one thing that is fully his job, something that will clearly go wrong if he does not do it. Then let it go wrong, just once. Do not fix it for him. Sit with him after and ask what he noticed.

Boys to Men Mentorship Camp is built around this kind of real, lived responsibility.
