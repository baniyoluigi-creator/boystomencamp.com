Our Story

Boys to Men Mentorship Camp began as a response to a simple, urgent observation: too many boys are moving into manhood without the mentorship, guidance, or safe space they need to do it well. Scroll down to read the full story, as told by our founder.

Read the full story ↓

As told by Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder

Where this began

I watched my family break when I was about four or five. My father and mother had a heated quarrel, and in anger he tried to cut my mother with a panga, but she escaped. He then cut their mattress into pieces and cut down the crops around our house. I still do not know what the quarrel was about. That week, my mother left with us.

I now love my father, and I miss him. For years, though, I judged him and wished I could ask him what happened. Now that I am older, I have seen my own imperfections So I no longer ask who was to blame. I ask different questions.

Why? Because every child is born innocent, happy, peaceful, loving and emotionally grounded, with a promising future. But as we grow, something happens. People say my father was brilliant, funny and full of life. Like most of us, he was flawed too. He got lost in alcohol. I am not saying he made no mistakes, and I am not saying he was the worst of men. He made choices, and he carries his share of the story. But choices are made in a setting. Dad grew up where alcohol was brewed and tobacco was grown and sold to pay school fees, and most people there started drinking and smoking as children. Many who grew up in such places did not end up broken, addicted or chaotic, so his surroundings cannot be the whole answer. Yet they may have made his road harder. What if he was more than his problem, both responsible for his choices and shaped by what he lived through? What if someone had mentored him early? If I had lived his life, would I have turned out better? I cannot say. But I do know that if we are not intentional, many young people today will end up far worse.

By Primary Two, I had lost both parents. I lived in several homes until I joined my mother’s elder brother, who raised me with great care. When he died suddenly in my Senior Two, his wife kept me. She is the amazing woman I call Mum. If life gave me kind people, what can I do for society? Maybe the camp is one way of thanking God.

Still, the fatherhood gap remained. Whatever good others see in me as a man, I learned from the few men who stepped in, and from many more I watched from a distance. A boy may lack a father who prepares him, but opportunities still exist. This camp is one. I could stay stuck in the questions and the pain, or I could help build a world where young men are better prepared for life.

What school did not teach us

Years after school, I had not made the progress I expected. At one point, I stayed seven months without a job, with a young family to care for. I felt stuck, and it humbled me. There was so much about adulthood that nobody had taught me, or that I had not taken time to learn. I concluded that education must be more than academic. Upbringing, mentorship and exposure must fill the gaps, so that young people understand their minds, manage anger, handle money, start businesses and lead well.

Many people I knew felt stuck like me, so we created our Life Readiness Programme to fill the gaps left by school and upbringing. The adult version, the Mindset, Entrepreneurship and Personal Development Masterclass (MEP), helps adults parent, lead and negotiate better, grow their careers and businesses, and prepare for life beyond formal employment.

Almost every MEP graduate said the same. “If only I had learned these things when I was younger, I would have avoided some mistakes and made more progress by now.” Most would add, “I wish my child could go through this earlier, so that they do not feel adulthood is a scam like we did. So when are you starting something similar for young people?” Boys to Men is the answer for boys.

What society expects of a man

Inspired by my childhood journey and the demands of parents, I knew something had to change. As boys grow up, society asks them, not always in words, “Are you ready?” We see six core expectations of manhood for which young men need preparation. We call them the 6Ps.

Purpose. He knows why he is here and gives value to others through his career or business.

Provide. From his honest earnings, he meets his own needs and those of his family and the people in his care.

Protect. He keeps his family and community safe and is never the one who harms.

Precede. He leads himself, then others, in words and by example.

Profess. He uses words well. What he says, and does not say, shapes the people he loves, so he speaks to build them and not to break them.

Procreate. He builds a family and raises children of good character.

Maybe, if my father had met mentors like these early, his story, and ours, would have gone differently. Should other young people walk the same road alone? I do not think so.

How the camp answers

Boys to Men is a seven day residential camp for boys and young men aged 8 to 23. They are grouped by age, each group with its own facilitators, so a boy of nine and a young man of twenty are never taught the same way. We have over 20 mentors, and each boy meets more than ten. The boys play games that carry a lesson, act out real situations, listen to stories, draw the life they want and ask any question they like. I wanted every boy to have the structure I never had, as a child and as an adult.

On the closing Saturday, parents and guardians join us for a session on parenting in a changing world, and the boys are crowned in a coronation ceremony, ready to become responsible, grounded and confident men.

V

Our Vision

To raise a generation of responsible, confident, and purpose-driven men who positively influence their families, communities, and society.

M

Our Mission

To provide transformational mentorship experiences that help boys discover their potential, develop their capabilities, and deploy their strengths for meaningful impact.

Our Values

What we will not compromise on

R

Respect

Every boy is treated with dignity, never shamed or singled out.

C

Calm

We coach, we do not command. Results come from a calm, respectful approach.

I

Integrity

We do what we say, especially with parents trusting us with their sons.

C

Community

A boy's growth does not end when camp ends, ours does not either.

Our Philosophy

Discover. Develop. Deploy.

Every boy carries potential. Potential alone does not create results. It has to move through three deliberate stages to become real progress.

1

Discover

Who he is: identity, purpose, self-awareness, and the potential already within him.

2

Develop

Who he can become: personal mastery, practical mastery, and people mastery.

3

Deploy

What he does with it: leading himself, leading others, and creating real impact.
