Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp, and is it right for my son?

It is a seven day residential mentorship camp for boys and young men aged 8 to 23. It covers what school and upbringing often leave out, such as thinking well, handling money, building the right networks, leading and speaking with confidence, managing anger, staying free from addiction and starting a business. Each boy also leaves with a clear vision and a plan. Read the full story of the camp.

How are boys grouped by age?

Each boy joins a cluster for his age once he arrives, so what he learns fits where he is. The clusters are Young Explorers (8 to 10), Path Finders (11 to 13), Rising Men (14 to 17) and Young Men (18 to 23). There is nothing to select in advance. See how the camp works.

When do the camps run?

Kampala runs every December, and the next one is 13 to 19 December 2026. Arua runs 10 to 16 January 2027. The Summer Camp runs every July in Kampala and the next one is 7 to 16 July 2027. Our most recent camp in Gulu ran in May 2026. See all intake dates.

How much does the camp cost?

Kampala costs UGX 500,000 or USD 150. Arua costs UGX 400,000 or USD 120. The Summer Camp costs USD 300, and a UGX equivalent can be arranged on request. There are no discounts for now, so we encourage parents to register early. Open the page for your intake to see the full details, Kampala, Arua or Summer Camp.

How do I register my son?

Go to Apply Now and complete the registration form with your details and your son's details. After you submit, you will receive an confirmation email straight away, and someone from our team will call you within 72 hours at the latest. If you are registering more than one boy, you can add each of them on the same form.

How do I pay, and what if I pay before registering?

A booking fee of UGX 50,000 (USD 50 for the Summer Camp) secures his place. It counts toward the total fee. It is not refunded at any time after it is paid, even if your son moves to another camp or is withdrawn. All payments go to our Centenary Bank account. You may send from your bank, MTN Mobile Money or Airtel Money, and the guide on the Payment Details page shows each step. Please do not send money to any phone number. If you have already paid, register him and tell us on the form, then upload your receipt on the Attach Proof of Payment page so we can match it to his name.

Can we pay in instalments?

Yes. Pay the booking fee to secure his place, then complete the balance in instalments. The full balance must be paid 7 days before the camp begins. See Payment Details.

Is any money refunded if my son is sent home from the camp?

No. The camp holds high standards of discipline, and we work step by step to help every boy put things right. If a boy is sent home because of indiscipline, or a serious breach of the camp guidelines, no part of the fee is refunded. For withdrawals before the camp, the refund terms are in the Information and Requirements document sent to you after payment.

What happens after I register?

You will receive an acknowledgement email, and our team will call you. Please ignore any step in that email that you have already done, such as payment or joining our WhatsApp group. Before the camp, we also call each boy to prepare him mentally, and he is free to call Eduardo or Patricia whenever he wants.

What should my son bring?

Once the booking fee is paid, we send you the full list of requirements within a week, so you have time to prepare. If it has not reached you, please contact us.

Is the camp safe, and who supervises the boys?

Every mentor is known to the team and supervised throughout the camp. Boys to Men is not a disciplinary or bootcamp style programme. Our approach is calm, coaching based mentorship. Parents can reach the team at any time, and the dedicated parents session and WhatsApp group keep you informed. Read our full Safeguarding commitment.

Who are the mentors and facilitators?

The camp is led by Banio Luiji Nobert, Patricia Kokora and Eduardo Fortunato Atibuni, who is the Managing Director of Boys to Men. We have a pool of more than 20 mentors, and during a camp the boys interact with several of them. Guest speakers also join us, for example for sessions on health, nutrition and a responsible lifestyle. Meet the mentors.

What happens during the camp?

Boys take part in mentorship conversations, leadership activities, team challenges, reflection sessions, life skills training and entrepreneurship exposure. The sessions are lively and participatory, with games, role plays, stories, group work and personal assignments, and they are built around our Discover, Develop, Deploy approach. See what seven days can mean for your son.

What happens after my son returns home?

Support continues. Every camp includes a session for parents, so you know what your son is working through and how to support him at home. The parents WhatsApp group and our learning blog keep the conversation going. The curriculum also has four levels, so a boy can return and move through them over about three to four years. Read more about life after the camp.

Are the Kampala, Arua and Gulu camps open to boys from the diaspora?

Yes. Every intake is open to boys and young men from anywhere, including those living abroad, and our camps have gathered participants from beyond Uganda. See the intakes.

Why is there a separate Summer Camp?

Many families in the diaspora cannot fit the Ugandan school holiday dates, and their summer holiday is longer. The July Summer Camp gives them more time. It is open to boys in Uganda too, and it suits Senior 6 vacationists who want to begin university life with more focus. Read about the Summer Camp.

Can boys living in Uganda join the Summer Camp, and how does it work from abroad?

Yes. The Summer Camp is open to boys and young men living or studying in Uganda, and to those joining from abroad. If you are travelling, apply for the Summer Camp and our team will follow up on travel guidance, timing and communication across time zones. See Summer Camp.

Do you have programmes for girls or for adults?

Yes. Girls Rising is the programme for girls, and it has no public intake yet. We are building a waiting list, so contact us if you would like your daughter added. The MEP Masterclass is the adult programme, for young men above 23 and for parents who want to grow. See the MEP Masterclass.

Who is behind the camp?

Boys to Men is an initiative of Potential Unexplored Ltd, a Uganda based organisation that works with institutions, young people and communities across Uganda and Africa on life readiness and organisational strengthening. Meet the team or read more about us.

How do I become a mentor, donor or partner organisation?

Visit the Support page to find the right path in. You can go straight to Become a Mentor, Donate or Sponsor or Partner as an Organisation.

Are Banio's books available to buy?

Banio is the author of twelve books. We are preparing a place on this website where they can be bought. Until then, please contact us and we will tell you how to get a copy. Read more about Banio.
