A Question Parents Ask Us

Are there Christian camps for boys in Uganda?

How to choose a faith based camp for your son in Uganda, what questions to ask about faith and values, and where the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp stands.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

Yes, several organisations in Uganda and the region run mentorship for boys with a faith foundation. Because faith matters deeply to many families, the wise step is to ask each camp plainly what it teaches and how it treats boys.

What to ask a faith based camp

Which tradition or denomination is it rooted in? Who teaches, and what training do they have? Is every boy expected to take part in every religious activity? How are boys from other traditions treated? Does what is taught match what you teach at home? How are boys corrected when they fail? Do the adults live what they teach?

Ask also what the camp teaches about character, because a boy can know many verses and still treat people badly. Faith is meant to show up in how he treats his mother, his sister, his neighbour and his employee.

Where the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp stands

The camp is built on values that most Ugandan families, whatever their faith, want in their sons. These are purpose, providing for others, protecting the weak, leading by example, speaking truthfully and raising children of good character. They are the six Ps of manhood we teach, and they sit alongside respect for family, community, the nation and Africa. Our founder's own leadership is faith-rooted, and his writing, facilitation and creative work often draw on it.

If faith is central to how you raise your son, say so when Eduardo or Patricia call you after you register. You may also telephone before you register. Ask how faith is reflected in the week, and judge the answer for yourself. You should expect a clear and honest answer from any camp.

Faith and character go together

Whatever you choose, choose a place where your son meets good men, hears the truth and sees it lived. A camp that adds to what you teach at home is a good partner. A camp that confuses him is not.
