How do I know a mentorship camp is genuine and well supervised?

How to check that a mentorship camp in Uganda is genuine and well supervised, from the people behind it to how money is handled.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

A genuine camp is easy to find, easy to question and easy to verify. A false one is usually the opposite.

Find the people

Real camps have real names and faces, and telephone numbers that someone answers. The Boys to Men Mentorship Camp lists its team, its mentors, each with an individual profile page, and its partners on boystomencamp.com. Eduardo Fortunato Atibuni is the Managing Director of Boys to Men. Patricia Kokora is a certified coach with MOGi Global Leadership School and an advocate for the boy child. Banio Luiji Nobert, the founder, has spent more than a decade training, coaching and mentoring people and institutions.

Check the organisation behind it

Boys to Men Mentorship Camp is not a standalone charity or company. It is an initiative of Potential Unexplored Ltd, a Ugandan company limited by guarantee. Over the last five years, our team has facilitated more than 130 training, coaching and mentorship sessions for institutions across Uganda and Africa. The camp has held eight intakes, with boys joining from five countries, and our work has reached more than 30,000 young people.

Check how money is handled

All camp payments go to one Centenary Bank account. We do not accept payment to personal phone numbers, and our website shows bank details only. If you wish to pay by MTN Mobile Money or Airtel Money, you send it to the bank account, and a step by step guide is on the website. You can then upload your receipt on the Attach Proof of Payment page. If anyone ever asks you to send camp money to a personal number, stop and telephone us.

Speak to other parents

The best check of all is a parent who has been. In a WhatsApp group of business people in Gulu City, parents shared what the camp had done for their sons. Martha Otim, who sent her sons to our Arua camp in May 2025, encouraged other parents from Soroti and Fort Portal to enrol theirs. Mr Ezati Nicholas told us his sons had been through the camp and were doing well, and that he planned to enrol another son. Ask Eduardo or Patricia whether a parent whose son has been is willing to speak with you.

Ask what supervision looks like

Ask who sleeps near the boys, who leads each session and how many mentors are present. Ask for the programme outline. The more openly a camp answers, the more you can trust it.
