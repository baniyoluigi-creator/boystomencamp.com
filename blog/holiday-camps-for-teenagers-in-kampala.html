A Question Parents Ask Us

Are there holiday camps for teenagers in Kampala?

Yes. The Boys to Men Mentorship Camp meets in Kampala from 13 to 19 December 2026. Here is what a teenager does there and what to check in any holiday camp.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

Yes. Every December the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp brings boys together in Kampala for seven days, and this year it runs from 13 to 19 December 2026. A boy of 13 joins the Path Finders, who are 11 to 13. Boys of 14 to 17 are the Rising Men, and young men of 18 to 23 form their own group.

What a teenager gets that a holiday class does not

Holiday classes fill the time with school subjects. A sports camp builds the body. A mentorship camp works on the person. It asks a boy who he is, who he wants to become, what he will do with his money, how he handles his anger and how he treats others.

Parents often tell us about the small things first. A boy sleeps on a double decker bed for the first time. Another cooks for the first time. One was so excited to cut cabbages as the boys cooked. These look small, yet they build independence, strength and resilience. One father in Australia told us his son has never been the same since the camp and still keeps in touch with the boys he met there.

Why the teenage years need this

Researchers who study families note that teenagers want independence faster than their ability to manage themselves grows. That gap is behind many arguments at home. A camp gives a teenager a safe place to practise independence while guides stay close.

What to look for in any holiday camp in Kampala

Ask who the adults are and how they were chosen. Ask what the boys will be taught and how. Ask how the camp will speak to your son before he arrives, and what happens after he goes home. Ask for a telephone number that someone answers.

For our camp, a member of the team will call you within 48 hours of registration, and each boy is called before the camp to prepare him. The fee for Kampala is UGX 500,000 or USD 150, and a booking fee of UGX 50,000 holds your son's place.

A word about the boy who is already doing well

Some parents think a mentorship camp is only for troubled boys. It is not. Many boys who come are doing well at school and at home, and their parents want to strengthen what is already good before life tests it.
