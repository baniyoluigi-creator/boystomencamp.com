How do I teach my son discipline and responsibility?

Practical ways to teach a boy discipline and responsibility in Uganda, from clear rules and real jobs to the habits parents model.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

Discipline is not something done to a boy. It is something he learns to do for himself. We call it self-leadership, and it begins long before a boy leads anyone else.

Keep the rules few and clear

Choose the rules that matter most. Say them in advance, explain why they exist, and keep them every day. A boy who knows exactly what is expected can be held to it fairly. A boy who guesses learns only to avoid being caught.

Be warm and firm at the same time

Research on parenting points again and again to the same combination, which is warmth plus clear limits. One review found that having even one parent who is both warm and firm is linked to better outcomes for teenagers, and that parental warmth helps across different communities. Harshness alone, and softness alone, both do worse.

Give him real jobs

Responsibility grows from using it. Let him cook, wash his clothes, look after younger ones and run an errand with real money. Let him budget a small allowance and feel what happens when it runs out. When he forgets something, let the natural consequence do much of the teaching, and then talk about it calmly afterwards.

Let him see it in you

Boys watch us more than they listen to us. If we are late, always on our phones, or break the rules we set, our words lose weight. Researchers found that teenagers accept guidance from parents who clearly live by their own values.

Change his environment for a while

We teach that what a boy is exposed to shapes what he believes, what he does and what results he gets. A camp changes that environment on purpose. At the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp, boys share sleeping quarters, cook, work in groups and take turns to lead. They meet the six Ps of manhood, which are Purpose, Provide, Protect, Precede, Profess and Procreate. Each one asks a boy to carry responsibility for himself and for others.

Many parents tell us the change they notice first is simple. Their son starts doing things without being asked.
