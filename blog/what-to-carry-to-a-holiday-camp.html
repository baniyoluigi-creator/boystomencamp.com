What should my child carry to a holiday camp?

What to pack for a residential holiday camp in Uganda, what to tell the camp about your son, and how the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp sends its list.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

The official list comes from the camp itself. For the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp, it is sent to the parent within a week after the booking fee is paid, so you do not need to guess.

General packing sense for a week away

While you wait for the official list, these are sensible things to prepare, and the official list always takes priority. Clothes that are comfortable for activities and a warm layer for cool evenings. Bedding and toiletries, as the list advises. A water bottle. A notebook and a pen, because boys write personal assignments and want to record what they learn. Any medicine he takes, clearly labelled with written instructions. Label every item with his name, since a camp full of boys will have many similar bags.

Keep it light. A boy should be able to carry his own bag.

What to tell the camp

Telling the camp more is always safer than telling it less. Mention allergies, medicines, medical conditions, special diets and any fears he has, including homesickness. Give a reachable emergency contact. Public health guides advise parents to ask how a camp collects this information and how it handles each child's needs. If your son is shy, has been bullied or has recently lost someone close to him, say so in confidence, so the mentors can look out for him.

What to leave at home

Leave expensive gadgets and valuables at home, as they are easily lost and can distract him. If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask before you pack it.

Pack his mind as well

Most boys arrive nervous. Our team calls each boy before the camp to explain what will happen and prepare him mentally. You can help by speaking positively about it, and by asking him what he hopes to learn. A boy who arrives curious and willing gets far more from the week.

When he returns, ask him what he learnt, what surprised him and what he plans to change. That conversation is often the first step of the change.
