A Question Parents Ask Us

Where can I take my teenage son for life skills training?

What life skills training for teenage boys should include in Uganda, how it should be taught, and how the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp teaches it.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

Look for a programme that teaches what school and home often leave out, and that lets your son practise instead of only listening. The Boys to Men Mentorship Camp is one place that does this for boys from 8 to 23 years old.

What life skills really are

When parents say life skills, they usually mean the things they wish someone had taught them. How to handle money, save and avoid debt. How to understand emotions and stop anger from costing friendships. How to find a purpose before choosing a course. How to choose friends wisely. How to build a good relationship and later a family. How to work and, where possible, to start something of his own.

We group these into three kinds of mastery. Personal mastery is about identity, potential, purpose and overcoming limiting beliefs. People mastery is about understanding and relating to others. Practical mastery is about competence and excellence in what he does.

How the training should be taught

Boys do not change because they were told to. They change when they play, argue, act, draw, tell their own stories and then do something with what they learnt. Our delivery is participatory and full of life, using games, role plays, stories, pictures, personal assignments, questions and group work. We draw on two respected participatory approaches, the ERI Approach and the GALS Methodology, and on a training manual of 20 topics that our team has built from years of experience.

UNESCO advises that this kind of education should come as sequenced lessons from pre-adolescence into young adulthood, not as a single talk. That is the thinking behind our four levels, which a boy can move through over roughly three to four years.

What your son leaves with

Each boy is guided towards a clear vision and a plan for his life, together with the habits that support them. The aim is a boy who leads himself well, so that he can later lead others well.

For schools and organisations

Schools and organisations can also bring this to their own young people. Our Holistic School Support Programme works with school leaders, teachers, parents and prefects, and we can train an organisation's own staff to deliver life readiness themselves.
