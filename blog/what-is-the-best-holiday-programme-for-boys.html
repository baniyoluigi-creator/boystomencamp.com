Which is the best mentorship camp for boys in Uganda?

How to choose a mentorship camp for your son in Uganda, what to look for, the questions to ask, and how the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp answers them.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

The best camp is the one that fits your son and can answer your questions plainly. No parent should choose a camp because of a loud poster or a long list of promises. So instead of naming a winner, this post gives you a way to judge any mentorship camp, including ours.

Look at who will speak to your son

A review of 70 studies, covering more than 25,000 young people, found that mentoring programmes do help. The effect was moderate, not magical. Programmes did better when they served more boys and used more male mentors. So ask who the mentors are, how many there will be, and what they have done with their own lives.

At the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp, a pool of more than twenty mentors work with the boys, and during a camp each boy meets several of them. Two people will also speak to you directly. Patricia Kokora is a certified coach and an advocate for the boy child. Eduardo Fortunato Atibuni is the Managing Director of Boys to Men.

Ask what is meant to change

A good camp can tell you what it is working towards. Ours has ten aims for every boy. They are a clear sense of identity, focus on school, freedom from bad habits and bad peer groups, healing from emotional wounds and limiting beliefs, a vision and a plan, money and enterprise skills, confidence and control of anger, respectful leadership including towards girls and women, brotherhood, and a healthy manhood that values family, community, the nation and Africa.

Then ask how it is taught. Our sessions use games, role plays, stories, drawings, personal assignments and group work, because boys learn what they practise.

Ask what happens after the last day

One week cannot finish a boy. The American Camp Association followed more than 5,000 families and found growth in self-esteem, friendships, independence and leadership. It also found that the value grows when boys return over several summers. That is why our curriculum runs across four levels over roughly three to four years, and why parents have their own session and a WhatsApp platform where the learning continues.

Ask who is behind it

Boys to Men is an initiative of Potential Unexplored Ltd, a Ugandan organisation that has facilitated more than 130 training, coaching and mentorship sessions over the last five years for institutions across Uganda and Africa. The camp has held eight intakes so far, and boys have joined us from five countries.

Then call, ask and compare

Read the Safeguarding page on our website. Telephone us. Speak to parents whose sons have been. A camp that is glad to be questioned is usually a camp worth considering.
