A Question Parents Ask Us

What can my son do during the school holidays in Uganda?

Ideas for how a boy can use the school holidays in Uganda, from responsibilities at home to work, reading, sports and a mentorship camp.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

A school holiday is a long stretch of free time, and free time shapes a boy as much as school does. The question is not only how to keep him busy. It is who he will have become by the day school reopens.

Give him something at home that is truly his

Let him own one part of family life for the whole holiday. He could cook one meal a week, look after the garden or the animals, manage the family shopping list, or fix what breaks. Ownership teaches faster than instruction. A boy who knows that people are waiting for his meal learns something no lecture can teach.

Let him earn and learn

Many families have a shop, a farm or a workshop where a boy can help for a few hours a day. Others have an uncle or neighbour with a skill worth learning. Let him handle a small amount of money and account for it. Ask him to read a book every two weeks and tell you what he learnt.

Pay attention to who he spends time with

A study of 2,500 boys and young men in Kampala found that 30.8 percent had tried alcohol and 10.5 percent had drunk in the previous 30 days. Those who had friends or siblings who drink, and those who were out of school, had higher odds of drinking. The study did not look at holidays, but it reminds us that the company a boy keeps matters a great deal. Idle weeks in poor company are worth planning around.

Keep his body and mind moving

Football, swimming, walking, church or mosque youth groups, volunteering and community service all give a boy a place to belong. Whatever you choose, agree a simple daily rhythm with him so that sleep, work, play and rest all have a place.

Include one thing that stretches him

Add one experience that takes him out of his usual routine. A week at a mentorship camp is one such experience. The American Camp Association found that camps help boys grow in independence, friendships and leadership.

Our camps meet in Kampala in December, Arua in January and Kampala again in July for the Summer Camp, which also suits boys who study abroad or live in the diaspora. Young men of 18 to 23, including Senior 6 vacationists waiting to start university, have a group of their own. Most programmes in Uganda stop at 17, and we have chosen to keep going.
