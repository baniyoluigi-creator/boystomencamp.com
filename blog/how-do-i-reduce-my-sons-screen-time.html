My son is always on his phone. What programme can help him?

What doctors and researchers say about teenagers and phones, how to set limits that work, and how a mentorship camp can help reset habits.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

A programme can help, but only if the habits at home change as well. Here is what doctors and researchers say works, and where a camp fits.

Look at what the phone is crowding out

The American Academy of Pediatrics does not give one screen time limit for all teenagers, because screens are used for school, friendships, games and more. It asks parents to look at what the phone is replacing, such as sleep, exercise, meals together and time outdoors, and to bring those things back in. Aim to crowd back in what matters, rather than only counting hours.

Protect his sleep first

The American Psychological Association advises that screen time should never cut into at least eight hours of sleep a night for teenagers. Reviews of sleep research recommend switching screens off at least an hour before bed, and keeping phones out of the bedroom, since even having a device nearby is linked to poorer sleep.

Make the plan with him

Doctors at the American Academy of Pediatrics say that restricting alone does not prevent unhealthy use, and that too much restriction can stop a teenager from building the skill of managing his own time. They suggest making the plan together, with agreed times and places for the phone. A researcher at the University of California, San Francisco found that taking a device away as punishment, or giving more screen time as a reward, did not work well. Clear limits and monitoring did help.

The same researcher notes that the strongest predictor of a child's screen use is the parent's own use. If we ask him to put the phone down at the table, we must put ours down too.

Know when it has become a problem

If the phone is damaging his sleep, mood, school work or friendships, it deserves serious attention, and a doctor or school counsellor can help.

Where a camp helps

A week at the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp gives a boy plenty to do with his hands, his body and his friends. He cooks, works in a team, listens to mentors and learns from other boys. It shows him that a life away from the screen can be full, and it gives your home a moment to reset the rules together.
