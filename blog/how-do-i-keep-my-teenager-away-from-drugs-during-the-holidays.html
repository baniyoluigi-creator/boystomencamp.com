How do I keep my teenager away from drugs during the holidays?

Practical steps for parents to protect a teenager from drugs and alcohol during long school holidays, including warning signs and when to get help.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

Long holidays create free time, and free time with no direction is when many boys are first offered drugs or alcohol. You cannot guard a teenager every hour, but you can lower the risk a lot.

Know where he is, who he is with and what he is doing. Meet his friends. Keep him busy with useful things, such as a skill, sport, work at home or a camp. Talk openly about drugs before he meets them, and explain the real dangers without exaggerating. Ask him what he thinks and listen.

Watch for warning signs. These can include sudden changes in friends, mood or sleep, falling school marks, missing money or belongings, red eyes, secretive behaviour, or new smells on his clothes. One sign alone does not prove anything, but several together deserve a calm conversation.

If you suspect a problem, speak to him calmly and not in anger, and seek help from a health worker, counsellor or trusted church or community leader. Shouting rarely helps.
