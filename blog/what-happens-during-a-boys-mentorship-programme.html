A Question We Hear Often

What happens during a boys' mentorship programme?

It is more intentional than parents expect, a mix of activity, honest conversation, real responsibility, and stillness. Here is what a day actually holds.

Parents often imagine either two extremes, either strict military style discipline, or loose free time with no structure at all. The truth sits in between, and it is more intentional than either.

Days are structured but not rigid. Boys move through physical activities, group challenges, and quiet reflection time, so both body and mind are engaged.

Conversations happen constantly, not just in scheduled sessions. Mentors talk with boys during meals, during walks, during downtime, because some of the most honest conversations happen when a boy is not expecting one.

“Some of the most honest conversations happen when a boy is not expecting one.”

Boys are given real responsibility, leading a task, solving a problem as a team, sometimes failing at it, and working through that failure with a mentor close by.

And there is space for stillness too, moments where a boy is simply allowed to think, without a phone, without noise, often for the first time in a long while.

By the end, most boys have opened up about something they had not planned to share, simply because the environment gave them room to.

Boys to Men Mentorship Camp is built around this full mix, activity, honest conversation, responsibility, and stillness.
