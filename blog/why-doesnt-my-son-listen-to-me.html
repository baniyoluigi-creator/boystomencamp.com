A Question We Hear Often

Why doesn't my son listen to me?

It is rarely just one reason. Here is what years of sitting with boys has taught us about why they stop listening, and what actually brings it back.

A mother came to us not long ago. She looked very tired. She said, “I talk and talk, but it feels like my words just bounce off him.”

We hear this a lot from parents, and the truth is, it is rarely just one reason.

Sometimes a boy stops listening because he feels talked at instead of talked to. Every conversation turns into a warning, so he starts closing his ears before you even speak.

“He was never incapable of listening. He was just done being managed.”

Sometimes it is because he no longer wants to be managed like a small child. Somewhere between age eleven and fourteen, something shifts in him. He wants to be reasoned with, not controlled.

Sometimes it is simpler than that. He has learned that if he stays quiet long enough, the talking will stop on its own, and nothing will actually change either way.

And sometimes, a boy has simply stopped believing his opinion matters in the conversation, so he has quietly given up trying to be part of it.

We see all of these at camp, in different boys, in different weeks. What works is almost always the same thing though. A mentor asks a real question, then waits for the real answer, without rushing to fix it or judge it. The boy who was silent at home will suddenly talk for a whole hour, because for once, someone is actually listening back.

Try this at home. Ask your son one honest question this week. Then stay quiet and let him answer fully, even if it surprises you. Watch which one of these reasons shows up in how he responds.

This is what we teach at Boys to Men Mentorship Camp. We show boys their voice matters, and slowly, listening starts to go both ways.
