A Question We Hear Often

Why is my teenage son pulling away from me?

This rarely has just one cause. Some of it is normal growth, some of it is protection. Here is how to tell the difference and what helps.

This is one of the most common worries parents bring to us, and it rarely has just one cause.

Sometimes it is simply normal growth. A teenage boy is meant to start separating from his parents a little, building his own identity apart from the family.

Sometimes it is because he feels judged more than understood at home, so pulling away feels safer than opening up and being corrected again.

“What consistently helps is not chasing a boy harder when he pulls away, but creating low pressure moments where he can come closer on his own terms.”

Sometimes he is testing new friendships and new interests, and does not yet know how to include his parents in that without feeling babied.

And sometimes, quietly, he is dealing with something he does not have words for yet, and distance is the only way he knows how to protect himself while he works through it.

We see all of these at camp. What consistently helps is not chasing a boy harder when he pulls away, but creating low pressure moments where he can come closer on his own terms, a walk, a shared task, a question with no wrong answer.

If your son has been distant, try spending time with him doing something, not talking directly about him, and see what opens up naturally.

Boys to Men Mentorship Camp gives boys exactly this kind of low pressure space to reconnect, with mentors and often, afterward, with their own parents too.
