A Question Parents Ask Us

Does a mentorship camp really change a teenager's behaviour?

What research and parents say about whether a mentorship camp changes a teenager's behaviour, what a camp can honestly promise, and what parents can do afterwards.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

Sometimes, yes, and parents often notice it first at home. But a week alone does not rebuild a boy, and a camp that promises otherwise should make you careful.

What research says

A review of 70 youth mentoring studies, involving more than 25,000 young people, found a real but moderate benefit. Its authors urge us to stay realistic about what mentoring can do. The American Camp Association, working with more than 5,000 families, found that parents, staff and children all reported growth in self-esteem, friendships, independence and leadership. It also found that the value of camp grows when boys return over several seasons or attend longer sessions.

What we have seen

A father in Australia told us that his son James has never been the same since he returned from the Kampala camp, and that James keeps in touch with boys he met there. A headteacher in Maracha, whose son attended, told Banio, "My son has not been the same since the camp." A young man named Etoma, who attended in Gulu, recognised our founder after a school session and said, "That camp changed my life immensely." At the Gulu camp, we met a boy who began withdrawn and found it hard to focus. By the closing day he was engaged, excited and open to new possibilities.

Stories are not statistics, and they do not guarantee your son's result. Yet they show the kind of change that is possible.

What we aim for

Our work aims at ten changes. They are identity, school focus, freedom from bad habits and bad peer groups, healing, a vision and plan, money and enterprise skills, confidence and control of anger, respectful leadership, brotherhood and a healthy manhood. We cannot promise all ten in seven days. We promise to point a boy in that direction and to stay in touch after the camp.

What makes the change last

Change lasts when the home supports it. Ask your son what he learnt. Practise one new habit with him. Join the parents session and the WhatsApp platform. Be patient, because growth under the soil does not show for a while. And consider the next level, since our curriculum runs across four levels over roughly three to four years.

The best time to plant a tree was twenty years ago. The second best time is now.
