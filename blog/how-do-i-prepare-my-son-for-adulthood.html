A Question Parents Ask Us

How do I prepare my son for manhood?

How to prepare a boy for manhood in Uganda. Talking about puberty, teaching responsibility, respect for women, and what the six Ps of manhood mean.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

Begin earlier than feels comfortable, and speak more plainly than anyone spoke to you.

Start with his body and his feelings

UNICEF advises parents to begin talking about body changes while a child is still in primary school, and to add more detail as he grows. In a study of boys in two parts of Ethiopia, boys said they do not talk to their fathers about body changes because fathers try to manage them by force. The same boys asked for more information from schools and homes. UNESCO adds that educating boys about puberty creates understanding that leads to a healthier social environment.

Many of us were told nothing. We can choose to do better.

Teach him what manhood asks

At the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp we teach the six Ps. A man has Purpose and knows why he is here. He is able to Provide for those who depend on him. He will Protect the weak and the vulnerable. He will Precede, which means he leads by going first. He will Profess, which means his words are honest and carry weight. And he will Procreate, which means he builds a family and raises children of good character.

Teach him respect for women and girls

UNICEF encourages parents to share household tasks, to talk openly about feelings and to model respect in what they do. Boys who see this learn it. At the camp we teach respectful leadership, including towards girls and women, because a healthy manhood starts with mutual respect between men and women.

Do not wait for perfect

Our founder, Banio Luiji Nobert, lost both parents while still a boy. He was raised by his mother's elder brother, and after his uncle died, by his uncle's wife, whom he still calls Mum. Whatever he became as a man, he learned in small pieces from men who stepped in and from others he watched from a distance. That gap is why the camp exists. We want every boy to have men who step in on purpose.

You do not have to be a perfect parent to prepare your son. You need to begin, stay honest and bring in good men to walk with him.
