A Question We Hear Often

How do I protect my son from bad friends?

You cannot fully control his friendships, but you can build the one thing that gives peer pressure less power over him. Here is how.

Parents often ask us this with real fear in their voice, and it is a fair fear, because friendships shape a boy more than almost anything else at this age.

Sometimes a boy chooses the wrong friends because he does not yet know his own worth, so he accepts whoever accepts him, good influence or bad.

Sometimes it is because he has not been taught how to say no without losing the friendship entirely, so he goes along with things to avoid being left out.

“You cannot fully control who your son spends time with, but you can build his sense of self so strongly that peer pressure has less power over him.”

Sometimes it is simply proximity, he has not yet met better options, so the friends around him are the only ones available.

And sometimes, honestly, the “bad friend” is showing him something he is missing at home, attention, excitement, or a feeling of being needed.

You cannot fully control who your son spends time with, but you can build his sense of self so strongly that peer pressure has less power over him. That is the real protection.

At camp, boys meet other boys who are also working on becoming better men, and many form friendships there that quietly replace the ones that were pulling them down.

Boys to Men Mentorship Camp gives your son a new circle built around growth, not pressure.
