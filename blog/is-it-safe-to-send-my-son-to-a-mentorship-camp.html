Is it safe to send my son to a camp in Uganda?

The questions every parent should ask before sending a son to a camp in Uganda, and how to check the Boys to Men Mentorship Camp for yourself.

By Banio Luiji Nobert, Founder of Boys to Men Mentorship Camp

It can be, and it is right to ask before you pay anything. Safety is not a feeling. It is a set of clear answers. Here are the questions to ask any camp, and how to put them to ours.

The questions to ask

Public health and child protection guides for parents agree on a core list. Who supervises the boys, and how many boys are there for each adult? How are staff chosen, checked and trained? What are the rules about one-to-one situations, photographs and phones? How are medical needs and medicines handled, and who responds if a boy falls ill? How will you be told in an emergency? Are the kitchen, food and water kept clean? How are boys grouped by age? What happens to a boy who is homesick? How can anyone report a concern, and what is done about it?

A camp that is proud of its safety will answer all of these without hesitation.

How to check us

Read the Safeguarding page on our website. Telephone us on +256 740 132 573 or +256 779 363 354 and put these questions to us. After you register, Eduardo or Patricia will call you within 72 hours at most, and each boy is also called before the camp so that he hears a friendly voice before he arrives. Your son may call Eduardo or Patricia whenever he wishes. Parents have their own session and a WhatsApp platform, so you are never far from what is happening.

Emotional safety matters too

A boy learns best when he feels safe to be honest. The American Camp Association found that camps improve the benefits for their campers by working on areas such as peer relationships, emotional safety and decision making. We take this seriously because boys cannot talk about their anger, their fears or their family wounds in a room where they feel judged.

Trust your instincts

Be careful of any camp that avoids your questions, hides who runs it, asks you to send money to a personal phone number or pressures you to decide quickly. A good camp welcomes your questions and gives you time to think.

If something about any camp worries you, ask again, ask someone else, or choose a different camp. Your son is worth the extra phone call.
